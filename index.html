Did Diwali change the air? Hourly PM2.5 in five Indian cities

1Question

Fireworks release large amounts of fine particles within a few evening hours, and Diwali also falls when post-monsoon weather can leave air stagnant over much of India. This note asks a narrow, checkable question: did modelled hourly PM2.5 in five cities rise during the Diwali window (7–9 November 2026) compared with the quiet week before, and is the rise concentrated in the evening and night? Lakshmi Puja, the main Diwali day, was on 8 November.

2Data and method

  • Cities: Solapur (home town), Pune and Mumbai (Maharashtra), Hyderabad and Delhi, each taken at one coordinate point.
  • Air quality: hourly PM2.5 in µg/m³ from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, which serves atmospheric-composition model output (Copernicus CAMS). These are modelled estimates, not ground-sensor readings.
  • Weather: hourly 10 m wind speed from the Open-Meteo forecast API, used as a first check on whether weather alone could explain a change.
  • Baseline: 25 October to 1 November, a stretch before the festival week. Window: 7–9 November (Choti Diwali to the day after Lakshmi Puja).
  • Metrics: mean PM2.5 in each period; percentage change = (window mean − baseline mean) ÷ baseline mean × 100; window peak (highest hour); and hours above 60 µg/m³, India’s 24-hour PM2.5 standard, used only as a reference line because it is a daily limit and the data are hourly.
  • Hour-of-day profile: PM2.5 varies through the day anyway, so Figure 2 compares each hour on 8 and 9 November with the average for the same hour across the baseline days.

4Limitations

  • Modelled, not measured. The values are model estimates on a grid several kilometres wide, so they smooth out local hotspots and may not match a nearby monitoring station.
  • Weather is a confounder. Wind, humidity and temperature changed over these weeks too. The wind column is a first check, not a control; a rise that coincides with Diwali is consistent with fireworks but does not isolate them.
  • Baseline choice matters. A different baseline gives different percentages. The settings in api/data.js can be changed to test this.
  • One festival, one year, five points. The note describes what happened here and does not support wider claims.

5Next steps

  1. Validate against ground monitors (CPCB / OpenAQ) in the same cities.
  2. Repeat for earlier Diwali dates, which move each year, to see whether the pattern repeats.
  3. Adjust for weather statistically instead of reading wind side by side.
